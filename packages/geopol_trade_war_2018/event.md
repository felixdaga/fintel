# US-China Trade War 2018-2019 — Curated Event Timeline

This file is the PIT-clamped chronology served to the agent. Only entries with
`entry_date < decision_date` are visible at each decision date. The agent sees
this via the `get_event_timeline` tool.

Sources for dates: USTR press releases, White House / Presidential statements,
CRS timelines, PIIE trade-war chronology, DOJ/CBC reporting on Meng/Kovrig/Spavor.

## Pre-escalation context

2017-08-14 — Trump signs memorandum directing USTR to investigate China's IP and technology-transfer practices under Section 301. USTR investigation initiated.
2017-11-08 — Trump visits Beijing; large package of commercial deals announced (many MOUs). No substantive concessions on structural IP / tech-transfer issues.
2018-01-22 — Trump approves Section 201 safeguard tariffs on solar panels and washing machines (China among suppliers hit). First major tariff action of the administration.

## 2018 escalation phase

2018-03-01 — Trump announces plan to impose 25% tariff on steel and 10% on aluminum imports (Section 232). Global scope; China is a smaller direct supplier share than some other exporters.
2018-03-02 — Trump tweets: "trade wars are good, and easy to win."
2018-03-08 — Trump signs Section 232 proclamations imposing the steel (25%) and aluminum (10%) tariffs.
2018-03-22 — USTR releases Section 301 report finding China's IP/tech-transfer practices unreasonable or discriminatory. Trump signs presidential memorandum directing tariffs on Chinese imports, a WTO case, and investment restrictions. USTR to publish a proposed tariff list.
2018-04-02 — China imposes retaliatory tariffs on about $3B of US goods (pork, fruit, nuts, wine, and related items) in response to Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs.
2018-04-03 — USTR publishes proposed Section 301 tariff list: ~1,300 Chinese product lines (~$50B), targeting aerospace, ICT, robotics, machinery and related sectors. Public comment process opens.
2018-04-04 — China announces proposed retaliatory tariffs on ~$50B of US goods (including soybeans, automobiles, and aircraft) in response to the Section 301 list.
2018-04-16 — US Commerce Department bans ZTE from buying US components (denial order). ZTE faces operational shutdown. Major non-tariff escalation.
2018-05-19 — US and China release joint statement after talks: China agrees to "significantly increase" purchases of US goods and services. Mnuchin says trade war is "on hold." Markets rally.
2018-05-29 — White House announces the US will proceed with Section 301 actions, including 25% tariffs on ~$50B of Chinese imports. Truce collapses.
2018-06-15 — USTR announces final two-stage $50B tariff plan: 25% on List 1 (~$34B, 818 lines, effective July 6) and List 2 (~$16B, later). China announces matching retaliation the same day.
2018-06-18 — Trump directs USTR to identify an additional ~$200B of Chinese imports for 10% tariffs if China retaliates against the $50B action.
2018-07-06 — List 1 tariffs take effect: US 25% on ~$34B of Chinese goods. China imposes matching ~$34B retaliation the same day.
2018-07-10 — USTR publishes proposed List 3: ~$200B of Chinese imports for an additional 10% tariff (~6,000 product lines). Public comment / hearing process begins.
2018-08-01 — At Trump's direction, USTR says it is considering raising the proposed List 3 rate from 10% to 25%.
2018-08-23 — List 2 tariffs take effect: US 25% on ~$16B (completing the $50B package). China imposes matching ~$16B retaliation.
2018-09-17 — Trump directs USTR to finalize List 3: 10% tariffs on ~$200B of Chinese imports effective Sept 24, scheduled to rise to 25% on Jan 1, 2019.
2018-09-18 — USTR publishes the finalized List 3 (5,745 lines after removing some consumer/electronics and other products from the July proposal).
2018-09-24 — List 3 takes effect at 10%. China imposes retaliatory tariffs (5–10%) on ~$60B of US goods. Combined US Section 301 coverage now ~$250B of Chinese imports.
2018-11-01 — Trump and Xi hold a phone call focused on trade; agree to meet at the G20 in Argentina.
2018-12-01 — G20 Buenos Aires dinner: Trump and Xi agree to a 90-day negotiation window. Planned Jan 1, 2019 hike of List 3 from 10% to 25% is suspended (tariffs remain at 10%). Same day: Canada arrests Huawei CFO Meng Wanzhou in Vancouver on a US provisional extradition request. China demands her release.
2018-12-10 — China detains Canadians Michael Kovrig and Michael Spavor (widely viewed as retaliation for the Meng arrest; Beijing denies linkage).

## 2019 negotiation and breakdown phase

2019-01-07 — Trade talks resume in Beijing (working / mid-level). China discusses increased purchases of US goods; structural issues remain open.
2019-01-28 — US DOJ unseals fraud and sanctions-related charges against Huawei and Meng; US formally requests Meng's extradition from Canada (deadline under Canadian law was Jan 30).
2019-01-30 — Lighthizer and Mnuchin in Beijing for high-level talks. Purchase commitments discussed; enforcement and structural IP/tech-transfer issues unresolved.
2019-02-14 — Another round of talks (through Feb 15). Progress reported on purchases; enforcement mechanism still disputed.
2019-02-24 — Trump tweets that talks have made substantial progress and further delays the scheduled List 3 hike from 10% to 25% (previously tied to the March 1 end of the 90-day window).
2019-03-01 — The original 90-day tariff-hike deadline passes without an increase (already delayed on Feb 24).
2019-04-03 — Intensive Washington talks between US and Chinese negotiators; both sides report progress on text, including enforcement language. No final deal signed.
2019-05-05 — Trump tweets that negotiations are going "too slowly" and that China is attempting to "renegotiate." Orders List 3 raised from 10% to 25% on May 10 and directs preparation of tariffs on remaining Chinese imports (~$300B).
2019-05-10 — List 3 tariff rate rises from 10% to 25%.
2019-05-13 — China announces it will raise retaliatory tariff rates on its ~$60B US-goods list (higher rates phased in; many effective June 1).
2019-05-15 — Trump signs Executive Order 13873 (securing information/communications technology supply chains). Commerce moves to restrict Huawei.
2019-05-16 — US Commerce Department adds Huawei and affiliates to the Entity List. US firms generally barred from selling to Huawei without a license.
2019-05-20 — Google suspends Huawei's access to Android updates / GMS; major US chip suppliers pause sales. Same day: Xi Jinping visits a rare-earth facility in Jiangxi (widely read as a signal on rare-earth leverage).
2019-05-31 — China announces it will establish an "unreliable entity list" targeting foreign firms that cut supplies to Chinese companies for non-commercial reasons.
2019-06-18 — Trump and Xi agree by phone to meet at the G20 in Osaka.
2019-06-29 — G20 Osaka: Trump and Xi agree to resume talks. Existing tariffs remain; no new tariffs "for the time being." Trump says he will ease some Huawei restrictions (Huawei remains on the Entity List; scope of any relief later unclear).
2019-07-30 — Trade talks in Shanghai (through July 31). Little progress reported.
2019-08-01 — After the Shanghai round, Trump announces 10% tariffs on the remaining ~$300B of Chinese goods (List 4), effective Sept 1.
2019-08-05 — After CNY breaks 7 per USD, US Treasury designates China a currency manipulator (first such designation since 1994).
2019-08-13 — USTR splits List 4: List 4A (~consumer/industrial mix) effective Sept 1; List 4B (including many cell phones, laptops, toys, apparel items) delayed to Dec 15. Some sensitive items (e.g. certain pharma, rare earths, critical minerals) excluded from List 4.
2019-08-23 — China announces retaliation on ~$75B of US goods (5–10%, split Sept 1 / Dec 15), including re-imposing tariffs on US autos. Trump orders US companies to "immediately start looking for an alternative to China" and directs a 5-point increase on the planned List 4 rate (to 15%) and threatened hikes on existing lists.
2019-08-30 — USTR formally implements List 4 rate at 15% (up from the originally announced 10%).
2019-09-01 — List 4A takes effect at 15% (~$110–125B of Chinese imports). China's first tranche of the ~$75B retaliation also takes effect.
2019-09-11 — China announces first tariff-exemption list for some US products (16 categories; industrial inputs / chemicals / related — not a broad farm rollback).
2019-09-13 — Chinese state media reports soybeans, pork, and some other farm goods will be excluded from additional retaliatory tariffs (goodwill signal ahead of talks).
2019-10-11 — After Washington talks, Trump announces a "Phase One" deal in principle: China to buy roughly $40–50B of US agricultural products annually; IP and currency issues also cited. Planned mid-October tariff hike delayed.
2019-12-13 — US and China announce Phase One text agreed. China commits to increase purchases of US goods and services by at least ~$200B over two years versus 2017 baselines. US suspends the Dec 15 List 4B tariffs; keeps most existing tariffs. No settlement of the Huawei/Meng issues in the deal.

## Post-deal (visible only after 2019-12-13)

2020-01-15 — Phase One Economic and Trade Agreement signed in Washington. Most Section 301 tariffs remain in place. Text addresses IP, tech transfer, financial services, agriculture, and currency, with a bilateral dispute-resolution mechanism; enforcement strength debated by observers.
